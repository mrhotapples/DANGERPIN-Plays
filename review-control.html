Control (2019)

  • Developer: Remedy Entertainment
  • Platform: PC
  • Genre: Third-person Action
  • Release date: October 29th, 2019
  • Played for: 32 hours
8/10

Overview

Control is a game I put off playing until the sequel, Control Resonant, released earlier this year and I said, "Man, I've gotta play the first game before I even think about playing the sequel!" Back in 2022, my buddy had actually gifted me a copy of Control on Steam, but I couldn't access the account he sent it to. Luckily, it's pretty cheap nowadays and I took advantage of that. Control ended up surprising me, and I can say with full clarity that it was a mistake to skip it until recently. Remedy have crafted an experience that brought me tears with its main narrative, had me up all night watching lore videos, and frustrated me a few more times than I'd like to admit. The worldbuilding drew me in, but it was Control's escalating power fantasy that had me unable to think about anything but throwing benches at people for a solid week of my life.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Psychic warrior power fantasy
  • Sleek visuals and high-interactivity
  • "Metroidvania"-style interconnected world

Cons

  • Pacing issues near the end
  • It's easy to get lost at first
  • Build options feel a little fluffy

Final Verdict

If you can stick it through the first hour or so, Control will blow your mind. The FBC is an interesting setting, and you'll no doubt be intrigued by the mystery of it all. You'll love the characters and end up really feeling for the peopple caught up in this disaster. But once you start flinging around chunks of concrete and levitating, you won't be able to put it down. Unfortunately, by the end of the game, low enemy variety, a lack of truly meaningful build crafting, and the repetitive nature of the loop will wear its welcome. Until that moment hits, you're in for one hell of a ride.
